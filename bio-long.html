Biography

Oriana Bandiera · Sir Anthony Atkinson Chair in Economics, London School of Economics

Oriana Bandiera is the Sir Anthony Atkinson Chair in Economics at the London School of Economics. She studies how people work together — in firms, public services and communities — and how the way work is organised affects opportunity and living standards.

A recurring question in her research is why some people are able to move into better jobs and more secure livelihoods while others remain stuck in poverty. Her work on poverty traps explores how a lack of productive assets can limit choices, and when well-designed interventions can help people move onto a different path. Other projects examine how friendships, incentives and a sense of purpose shape behaviour at work, and how organisations can attract and motivate people to deliver public services.

She approaches these questions through a combination of economic theory, detailed data and field experiments, often working closely with researchers and organisations in the places being studied. She is based in LSE’s Department of Economics and STICERD.

Her contributions have been recognised with the European Economic Association’s Birgit Grodal Award (2026), as well as the Yrjö Jahnsson Award (shared with Imran Rasul), the Ester Boserup Prize and the Carlo Alberto Medal. She served as President of the European Economic Association in 2022 and as Co-Editor of Econometrica from 2019 to 2023. She is a Fellow of the British Academy and the Econometric Society, and enjoys working with doctoral students as they develop their own research careers.
